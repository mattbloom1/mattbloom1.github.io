9.5/14pt is the sweet spot for a two-column 8.5 × 11. At .6in margins and a .32in gutter each column is 3.49in — roughly 66 characters at 9.5pt, right in the comfortable 55–75 range.

The CMYK values above are naive arithmetic conversions, not profile conversions — treat them as starting points only. The real numbers come from converting against your printer's ICC profile (commonly US Web Coated SWOP v2). Ask them which profile and which PDF/X flavour they want before you build the file.

Light text on a dark ground gains apparent weight in print — ink spreads slightly into the paper. Reversed body copy usually needs to go up one weight step and one size step versus the same text on white. Below about 8pt, reversed Raleway Light starts to fill in on uncoated stock.
Black text — the single biggest press gotcha
100% K (correct)Four bedrooms over two floors, with a private landscaped terrace.
Navy (correct — brand)Four bedrooms over two floors, with a private landscaped terrace.
Body copy must end up as 100% K only — a single ink. A naive RGB→CMYK conversion builds pure black from all four plates, and small text in four-colour black shows coloured fringing when the plates are a hair out of register. A conversion-settings problem rather than a design one, but the thing most likely to bite us — worth proving on a physical proof first.
